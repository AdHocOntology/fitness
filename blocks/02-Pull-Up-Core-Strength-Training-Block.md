## Goals

- Progress from zero clean bodyweight pull-ups toward five consecutive repetitions. 
- Increase core stability, endurance and strength 
- Maintain posterior-chain gains and include quadriceps training.
- Continue chest and arm development while establishing consistent shoulder work.
### Mechanism

- Eight full-range assisted pull-up sets across two weekly sessions, supported by three rowing sets.
- Eleven direct core sets across all three main sessions.
- Six quad sets and six hamstring sets weekly, including three hip-hinge sets.
- Ten chest sets, eleven direct shoulder sets and two isolation sets each for biceps and triceps in the main sessions; additional quad, shoulder, arm and core work in the optional session.
- Three complete sessions, with a fourth that adds volume without containing essential work.

The allocation prioritizes pull-up practice and consistent core training while supporting continued chest and arm development. D supplies extra quad, shoulder, arm and core work; the pull-up and core priorities are covered in A–C. These are starting allocations for this block, rather than a requirement to bring every muscle to the same weekly set count.

## Weekly

Allow roughly 60–75 minutes per main session, including warm-ups; actual time depends on equipment availability and unilateral work. Rest about 2–3 minutes between pull-up and other compound sets, and 60–90 seconds between accessory sets, taking longer if needed to preserve technique. Run A, B and C in order, preferably with a recovery day between them. Days remain flexible.

**Unilateral counting:** two sets per side count as two paired sets here, not four. Each side receives two sets, but both sides must be completed. This applies to dead bugs, side planks, Pallof presses and unilateral accessory work. The extra execution time still matters.

Before A and C, perform 1–2 easy scapular pull-up preparation sets; these are excluded from working-set totals. Use machine or secure foot assistance until a band is available. Keep assistance/setup consistent, leave 1–2 clean reps in reserve, and reduce assistance slightly once all four sets reach eight clean reps.

The whole D session remains optional; when performed in a full-volume week, it contains all 18 sets above. Allow roughly 40–60 minutes. Keep it less demanding than the main sessions by using controlled accessory work and avoiding failure. Schedule it so shoulder and arm fatigue does not impair the next pull-up session; skip D if recovery or time is limited.
