## Goals

- Progress from zero clean bodyweight pull-ups toward five consecutive repetitions. 
- Increase core stability, endurance and strength 
- Maintain posterior-chain gains and include quadriceps training.
- Maintain chest and arm development while beginning consistent shoulder work.
### Mechanism

- Eight band-assisted pull-up sets across two weekly sessions, supported by three rowing sets.
- Eleven direct core sets across all three main sessions.
- Six quad sets and six hamstring sets weekly, including three hip-hinge sets.
- Six chest sets and eleven direct shoulder sets in the main sessions; additional shoulder and arm work in the optional session.
- Three complete sessions, with a fourth that adds volume without containing essential work.

The extra volume prioritizes pull-up practice and consistent core training. Chest, legs and shoulders receive smaller increases to support the other goals. D supplies extra shoulder work and direct arm training; the pull-up and core priorities are covered in A–C. These are starting allocations for this block, rather than a requirement to bring every muscle to the same weekly set count.

## Weekly

Allow roughly 60–75 minutes per main session, including warm-ups; actual time depends on equipment availability and unilateral work. Rest about 2–3 minutes between pull-up and other compound sets, and 60–90 seconds between accessory sets, taking longer if needed to preserve technique. Run A, B and C in order, preferably with a recovery day between them. Days remain flexible.

**Unilateral counting:** two sets per side count as two paired sets here, not four. Each side receives two sets, but both sides must be completed. This applies to dead bugs, side planks, Pallof presses and unilateral accessory work. The extra execution time still matters.

The whole D session remains optional; when performed in a full-volume week, it contains all 16 sets above. Allow roughly 40–60 minutes. Keep it less demanding than the main sessions by using controlled accessory work and avoiding failure. Schedule it so shoulder and arm fatigue does not impair the next pull-up session; skip D if recovery or time is limited.
